## ⛔ THE ONE DECISION WAITING FOR THE BUILDER

**Can a stopped write be told apart from a disconnect, and if so, where does it land?**

`src/channel/transfer.rs:93` is `Err(_) => SendOutcome::Disconnected` — a wildcard that erases every
distinct write failure into one variant. That is the class 24x named at `src/kernel/peer.rs:118`,
two hundred lines from where the reader does it right.

Killing it needs a `SendOutcome::Shutdown`, which cascades into `src/kernel/address.rs:127` — an
exhaustive match over `{Ok, Disconnected}` with no wildcard, whose `ConnectFail` offers only:

- `Refused` — retryable, *"the server may come up"*
- `Rejected` — identity mismatch, not retryable
- `Failed` — an io error carrying its reason

**None of those honestly means "this process is stopping."** Extending it reaches the wat-facing
`ConnectOutcome` (the arc-278 connect' wall). A rider hit this as a STOP trigger and reverted rather
than fabricate a landing spot; that was correct.

**The consequence, so the cost is visible:** a stop reaching `address.rs` today is reported as
`Refused` — telling a dialer to RETRY a process that is shutting down — in the very arm whose own
comment gets the outcome-wall discipline right. Not a regression (every write failure already landed
there), but a live lie.

**Do not settle this alone.** It is a design call the builder deferred explicitly.

## The lessons this session paid for

**The tie-break does not transfer from reader to writer.** I wrote "shutdown wins ties" into the #5
brief, lifted from the reader without asking whether it applies. A READ racing a stop has nothing
left to read; a WRITE racing a stop may be the **dying declaration** (R51 — `eprintln` is "the last
thing I'll say"). Preferring shutdown made a stopped process unable to say why it stopped. Caught by
`sigterm_reaches_a_program_blocked_on_stdin` going 0 → 1, not by me. **A rule copied from a sibling
subsystem owes the same grounding as a rule invented from scratch.**

**A stash differential converts a hypothesis into a fact in one run.** That failure appeared
alongside my own work; the stash said *passes without, fails with*, and reproducing it ISOLATED
killed the load-flake hypothesis before it could comfort me. Do this BEFORE diagnosing anything that
surfaced next to your own change.

**A vacuous gate hides its own bugs too.** Renaming a file broke a `read_to_string` path and exposed
`probe_arc214_stone82_*`, which had been asserting properties of a service deleted in Phase 3 —
green on nothing, indefinitely. Re-specimened at the live subject it went red at once, and the cause
was the GATE: `.contains(":Add")` matches `:wat::kernel::Address<…>`. The false positive had been
unreachable for exactly as long as the vacuum was. Saved as
`feedback_a_vacuous_gate_hides_its_own_bugs`.

**The escape hatch is always cheaper than the fix, and I reached for it twice.** First an `expect`
sugar to shrink a 76-site migration (a difficulty argument, and it would have mass-added an escape
hatch nobody deliberated); then `rune:lint` to quiet a lint whose own doc says a non-EDN literal is a
DETECTOR bug, "never a reason written and moved on." The builder cut the first; the lint's
documentation cut the second.

**Three greps that could not reach the thing** (a `grep -v '^./…'` filter against output with no
`./` prefix, three separate times), and **three piped-exit misreads** (`$?` after a pipe;
